SPOT THE SCAM - CLASS QUIZ
Eight questions covering email phishing, smishing, vishing, and quishing.
Suggested time: 30 seconds. One correct answer per question.

1. Your account closes today
An email asks you to reply with your password and login code to keep your campus account.
What is the safest next step?
A. Reply with both details
B. Contact IT using the official help desk
C. Use the email link to log in
D. Send only the code
Correct answer: B
Explanation: Do not share login secrets in response to an unexpected request. Verify through a trusted channel.

2. Pay a small redelivery fee
A text asks for $1.50 through a link to release a package. You are expecting a delivery.
What is the safest next step?
A. Pay because a package is expected
B. Reply with a card number
C. Check the carrier’s official app
D. Open the link to inspect the payment form
Correct answer: C
Explanation: An expected delivery does not prove this particular message is genuine. Check independently.

3. Read out your login code
A caller says your account is under attack and asks you to read out a code that just arrived.
What is the safest next step?
A. Read the code to stop the attack
B. Trust the caller ID
C. Ask the caller to repeat your name
D. Hang up and call your bank using a trusted number
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Caller ID and personal details can be misleading. Do not share a login code with an unexpected caller.

4. A sticker covers the payment code
A new QR-code sticker covers an older payment sign and opens an unfamiliar payment address.
What is the safest next step?
A. Use the operator’s official app or payment machine
B. Pay because the sign is in a public place
C. Trust any page with a padlock
D. Enter a different card to test the site
Correct answer: A
Explanation: A code can be replaced. A padlock shows an encrypted connection, not that the business is legitimate.

5. Your book is due tomorrow
An expected library reminder arrives. It does not ask for a password or payment.
What is the safest next step?
A. All emails are scams
B. Reply with a password
C. Check loans through your usual library app
D. Forward it without reading
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Not every message is phishing. Use a trusted app or bookmark when checking.

6. You won a gift card
A text promises a prize and asks you to pay a processing fee through an unfamiliar link.
What is the safest next step?
A. Pay the small fee
B. Avoid the link and report the text
C. Send a photo of your bank card
D. Reply to ask which card to use
Correct answer: B
Explanation: Unexpected rewards and payment requests are warning signs. Report the text through your messaging app.

7. Install a remote-access tool
An unexpected caller says your laptop is infected and tells you to install remote-access software.
What is the safest next step?
A. Install it immediately
B. Give them your password instead
C. Trust them if they know your name
D. End the call and contact your real IT team
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Unexpected remote-access requests deserve independent verification. Do not give the caller control of your device.

8. Your presenter shares a game code
Your presenter shows a QR code for Kahoot. What is a sensible habit before opening its destination?
What is the safest next step?
A. Assume every QR code is malicious
B. Check the source and the destination URL
C. Enter any details the page requests
D. Trust a QR code because it looks professional
Correct answer: B
Explanation: QR codes are not inherently malicious. Check who provided the code and where it leads.
